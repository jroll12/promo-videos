# Storylines (three different films, then one)

Source: `Apologist AI — Platform.pdf` (one-page ministry sheet, Drive, modified 2026-10-29), the Apologist AI logo files
in Drive (`apologist-ai.ai`, `apologist-ai-light.ai`), the Brand Quick Reference (Confluence), and web search.
apologistai.com could not be read (blocked in this environment).

**What the film must get across, in the brochure's order:**
1. Need: people bring their deepest questions online at 2 a.m., in their own language, sometimes where it isn't safe to
   ask out loud. Your team can't answer them all. Generic AI hedges, misquotes Scripture, or contradicts what you teach.
2. Mechanism: a faithful, branded agent without the build. It answers from your own teaching, any hour, any language,
   cites its sources, and passes people to your team when they need a real person.
3. Proof: #1 Christian AI platform on The Gospel Coalition's 2026 AI Christian Benchmark; trusted by Ligonier, Got
   Questions and others; 2.5M+ faith questions, 600K+ conversations, 205+ countries, 192 languages, 19 ministries.
4. Ask: "We run your AI infrastructure so you can focus on the people you serve." Book a quick intro call
   (calendly.com/apologist-ai/intro) or visit apologistai.com. Tagline: "Your mission. Our platform. Christ made known."

Audience: ministry leaders and staff (the brochure is the B2B sheet). House rule: never lead with the product.

## A. "Anyone who asks" (recommended)
Device: one seeker's question, carried through a ministry's own website chat from 2 a.m. to a real person.
Form: the product's UI doing the job, on the brochure's navy, indigo and lavender grounds.
The viewer sees a question typed into a ministry's chat in the middle of the night, hears 1 Peter 3:15, then watches
that one chat become one of a field of questions the team cannot reach. The same chat then answers, with Scripture,
a cited source and a handoff to a person, so the question from frame 0 is answered by about 0:35.
- First two seconds: a chat window on a dark screen, "2:07 AM" in its header, the words "Is there any hope for someone
  like me?" already typed, cursor blinking. Nothing else moves yet.
- Beats: hook (the question) -> verse (the calling: anyone who asks) -> tension (the questions never stop; your team
  can't answer them all; generic AI hedges, misquotes, contradicts) -> reveal (Apologist AI, a branded agent) -> proof of
  mechanism (answer from your teaching, sources, any language, handoff) -> claim (#1 on TGC 2026, trusted by, the stat
  band) -> end card (tagline, book a call, apologistai.com, QR).
- Look: the brochure's navy #111828 top, indigo #1D1640 band, lavender #F7F6FB / #ECE7FB grounds; Inter ExtraBold
  headlines; purple #7137FF used only for the cross, the "#1" and the primary button.
- Sound: narration; a send pop on the question; soft notifications as the field fills; clicks on chips; a whoosh at
  each cut; one riser into "#1". No music (no licensed music source here).

## B. "The morning after"
Device: a ministry director's 8 a.m. review of last night's conversations.
Form: a dashboard and transcript reader, light UI, documentary pace.
The viewer sees what happened overnight: questions from many countries, each answered with citations, and the few that
were routed to the team with a flag. It sells to the buyer's eye (control, review, trust) rather than the seeker's moment.
- First two seconds: a sunrise-lit laptop screen with an overnight summary card, the first conversation already open.
- Beats: hook (what happened while you slept) -> tension (you could not have answered these) -> reveal (your branded
  agent did, in your words) -> proof (TGC #1, stats) -> claim -> end card.
- Look: light lavender UI, indigo panels, Inter; one purple accent.
- Sound: narration, soft UI clicks, a page-turn whoosh per conversation.
- Why not first: it needs a convincing mock of the real admin dashboard, and I have no screenshots of it; a guessed
  dashboard would misrepresent the product. It also opens on the product, which the house rules advise against.

## C. "To anyone who asks" (the bold one)
Device: 1 Peter 3:15 taken apart word by word, each phrase opening onto a piece of the platform.
Form: kinetic typography only, no UI, on the brochure's dark textured ground with its faint cross.
"Always" becomes a clock running through the night; "anyone" becomes a wall of question bubbles in many scripts; "who
asks you" narrows to your ministry's name; "a reason for the hope" becomes a cited answer. It is a meditation that
ends on the tagline and the ask.
- First two seconds: the single word "Always" in Inter ExtraBold on navy, a clock hand sweeping behind it.
- Beats: verse phrase -> its product meaning, five times -> proof card -> tagline -> end card.
- Look: type only, the brochure's palette, JetBrains Mono for references.
- Sound: narration, a low sustained tone under the verse (would need a music source), type ticks.
- Why not first: beautiful but abstract for a buyer who needs to see the chat work, and a scripture-led opening
  reads as a devotional on a feed.

**Why A wins:** it opens on a person (house rule), shows the mechanism a ministry is buying (answers from its own
teaching, sources, handoff) instead of describing it, and carries the brochure's own verse and tagline.
